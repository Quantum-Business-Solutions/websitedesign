Our recommendation

We would build Clean

stretch: a plain, fast, high-contrast catalogue built for dealers and specifiers scanning for a product

The audience is dealers and specifiers

They scan a catalogue to find a panel or a module. Clean puts the product names and categories first and stays out of the way.

DMP's own look is high contrast

Yellow and black on white is already DMP's brand. Clean lets it read as confident rather than loud.

The catalogue is large

dmp.com lists hundreds of product pages and a deep library. A plain, fast layout carries that better than a showy one.

The one thing we would change: Confirm the yellow, replace the theme typefaces with DMP's own (Gotham is licensed) and swap in any new photography DMP has.

What we are treating as fixed

What your site and brand profile already say

There is no design brief from DMP yet. This version is written from dmp.com itself, read on 9 November 2026: its history, certifications, support lines, product catalogue, news and photography, in DMP's own words wherever a claim is DMP's to make. Tell us where it is wrong; your words outrank ours.

  • DMP sells through authorized dealers and distributors, so the site's first job is dealers and specifiers: find the product, get the spec sheet, place the order, reach a person.
  • DMP is a current Quantum client with a HubSpot CRM, marketing, sales and service launch under way, so the site has to feed that: forms that create records and one place for dealers to start.
  • DMP's own brand is yellow, black and beige. The previews use that palette, DMP's real logo and DMP's own photography, so what you see is closer to the finished site than a mood board.

What we found

And what we would do about it

  • Search: dmp.com earns about 24,493 organic visits a month on 7,462 keywords, but 66 percent of that is people already searching the brand. DMP appeared in none of nine buyer searches from Springfield and was named in none of six AI answers (Semrush and live searches, 13 September 2026).
  • On the live www.dmp.com every page carries a title, one H1, a canonical and Organization markup with the founding date, phone and address. Product pages carry no Product markup, no FAQ markup and no question headings, and there are no sameAs links and no llms.txt.
  • The category terms are open: access control, fire alarm and video surveillance searches go to distributors and integrators because DMP's pages are written for people who already chose a part.
  • The apex dmp.com now answers with a 301 to http://www.dmp.com first, then to https, so a visitor makes an extra hop through plain HTTP. robots.txt does not name any AI crawler, and a second sitemap line points at a gforce.www.dmp.com host.
  • Correction: the September analysis crawled only the redirecting apex and reported no robots.txt, sitemap, title, H1, canonical or structured data. All of those exist on www. Findings about Product markup, FAQ markup and sameAs were re-checked on 9 November and stand.
  • Strengths to keep: 50 years of history, a real manufacturing story, SOC 2 Type II examinations, 14 product categories and a deep library of spec sheets and guides.

To confirm with you

Ten things we wrote as a draft, not a fact

Each of these appears on the pages. None is built until you confirm or correct it.

  • How the product catalogue is built. dmp.com has hundreds of product pages (the sitemap lists panels, keypads, modules, readers, cameras and accessories by part number). HubSpot's page limits depend on the tier, so we need DMP's tier before deciding between building every product page in HubSpot and keeping the catalogue on its current system behind the new front door.
  • The promise line. The home page says 'Security systems, built in Springfield', which is DMP's founding story, not a confirmed tagline.
  • Typefaces. DMP uses Gotham. The previews use the Quantum theme typefaces; Gotham needs a licence for the real site.
  • Photography and logos. Images come from dmp.com, as does the logo. They are used here for a private preview. Confirm rights, and supply anything newer.
  • Milestone details. The timeline repeats DMP's own history list. Photos beside 1975, 1993, 2005, 2021 and 2025 come from dmp.com's history and about pages; confirm the years against the originals.
  • Claims taken from DMP's own announcements: the XR Series being 32 times faster, the XR650 award, JamAlert's UL certification, and the jewelry store coverage mentioned in the JamAlert graphic. Each needs DMP's approval before it goes live in a headline.
  • Industries. Eight of eleven are given a page, and the industry pages are written from the product catalogue because the industries page on dmp.com was not reachable. Industrial manufacturing, multi-tenant and residential are left out.
  • Social profiles. None are linked on dmp.com, so none appear. We recommend adding LinkedIn, YouTube and Crunchbase to the markup.
  • Dealer routes. A dealer locator, a become-a-dealer path and a dealer login were not found on dmp.com, so none are drawn. Confirm what dealers and specifiers need to start.
  • The associations strip. The logos are those shown on dmp.com's About page. Confirm membership status for each before showing them as a row of badges.
  • The apex redirect, the gforce.www.dmp.com sitemap line and the buy.dmp.com ordering site are out of scope for the front-end build but affect search and the hand-off.

The plan

From a choice to a live site

Days 1 to 10

Serve the site at one canonical host with a single HTTPS 301, name the crawlers in robots.txt, publish llms.txt, and add sameAs links and a Springfield LocalBusiness block to the Organization markup.

Days 10 to 30

Confirm the items below, decide how the product catalogue is built (see the first item), and sign off copy for each of the eight system lines.

Days 30 to 45

Build the eight system pages with question headings, FAQ markup, product lists and an enquiry form on each.

Days 45 to 60

Build the Springfield page and the about and proof pages, and add Product markup to the catalogue pages.

Days 60 to 90

Publish the first three answer posts against access control, fire alarm and video surveillance questions, and the news and resources hub.

Day 90

Re-run the same nine Springfield searches, the same six AI questions and the same page audit against the baseline.
