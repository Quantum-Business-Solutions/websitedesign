Build it on one platform

Your website, your CRM and your dealers, on the same platform.

DMP is launching HubSpot CRM, marketing, sales and service with us. This page explains why the website belongs there too, what the build looks like, where HubSpot is not the strongest tool, and the questions worth putting to any bidder, including us.

The short version

Three reasons, and the honest limits

1

One record for every visitor

On HubSpot, forms, tracked downloads and page views land on the contact and company record the moment they happen. A dealer who reads a product page, then asks for a quote, is one timeline. On a separate CMS, that link is a connector someone has to build, secure and keep working.

2

The catalogue fits

dmp.com's sitemap lists 369 product pages and 26 categories. HubSpot's HubDB holds 10,000 rows per table, and pages generated from it do not count against the website page limit. A product table with a filterable listing is a normal build, not a stretch.

3

Less to look after

Hosting, the CDN and platform security updates are HubSpot's job, and its hosting providers are audited to SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001. Editors work in a drag-and-drop editor on theme modules we build once. There are no plugins to patch.

The honest limits. HubDB is a flat table, not a relational database, and faceted filtering is something we build rather than switch on. If DMP's long-term plan is a configurator or a compatibility matrix driven from a product information system, a developer-led CMS is the stronger base. The comparison below says where.
The real problem

Untangling the maze

dmp.com carries about 2,500 visible pages, plus an employee intranet, the Buy site, Marketing Central and a few connected sites. People describe it as a maze. A rebuild on any platform can make it look better. Only a change in structure makes it easier.

1

Three front doors, not one menu

Dealers, specifiers and building owners each start on a page built for them, with the same navigation underneath. The home page in each direction shows how.

2

Pages by design, products by data

Roughly a hundred designed pages cover the systems, industries, support, training, partners and company. The 369 product pages come from one table and one template, and documents live in one library instead of hundreds of pages.

3

Connected sites, one sign-in

Dealers reach the Buy site, Marketing Central and dealer-only documents through one sign-in tied to their CRM record (memberships, tier to confirm). The employee intranet stays separate.

A plan, not a promise yet. How Marketing Central and the Buy site connect depends on how they are built today. We would map them in the first two weeks, before any design is signed off.

How we stage it

The site is built and reviewed on a HubSpot address marked noindex, then pinned to dmp.com on launch day. Nothing indexable changes until the redirect map has been tested.

How we protect what ranks today

A redirect map covers every URL that earns traffic, loaded in bulk (HubSpot takes up to 500 per CSV, 140 characters per URL). Product page addresses are kept wherever possible, lowercase.

Consent text

Every form carries consent wording and a link to the privacy page, and the newsletter and lead magnet forms capture marketing consent separately from the request. The wording itself is DMP's to approve (to confirm with DMP's legal contact).

Notify teams, not people

Form notifications go to teams or shared mailboxes, not to individual user IDs. A person who leaves or changes role should not silently stop a dealer application from reaching anyone.

Measurement plan

What we count, and when we look

Conversion events are named before build so every one is tracked from day one. Reporting combines GA4 with HubSpot attribution, so a visit, a form and a deal read as one story. No baseline numbers are stated here; the baseline is recorded before launch and the reads compare against it.

Named events

  • Form submits, one per intent (the eight forms above)
  • Spec pack download
  • Dealer portal sign-in clicks
  • buy.dmp.com outbound clicks
  • Phone taps
  • Search use
  • Finder use
  • Assessment completion

Source tagging

Each form writes its hidden source field to the contact. Campaign links carry UTM tags, so GA4 and HubSpot attribution agree on where a lead came from. A short tagging guide goes to everyone who posts a link.

Reviews at 30, 60 and 90 days

The same cadence as the standard: a baseline on day 1, then reads at 30, 60 and 90 days after launch covering traffic, key pages, forms and the money path, plus AI-answer tracking. Each read lists what to change next.

Side by side

HubSpot CMS and Craft CMS, fairly

dmp.com runs on Craft CMS today, and another agency may propose staying there. Craft is a good product. This is where each one is stronger, from the vendors' own pages.

Where Craft would be the better choice. A catalogue with deep relationships (compatibility, accessories, configurators), a fully headless front end, or a product information system as the source of truth. If the site is mostly marketing, a catalogue, documentation and lead capture that feed a CRM, HubSpot is simpler to run and to own.

Risks we would manage on HubSpot

What could go wrong

  • Catalogue architecture. Settle table design, filters and the 10-template limit before any page is drawn.
  • Tier. Dynamic pages and memberships need Content Hub Professional or Enterprise; confirm what DMP holds.
  • Lowercase paths. HubDB dynamic page paths must be lowercase, so mixed-case product URLs need mapping.
  • File URLs. Files cannot be redirected, so document names stay as they are.
  • Hand edits. Protect editors' changes from being overwritten by a rebuild.
What we learned on three earlier HubSpot builds

Five lessons, and how we pre-empt each

These come from three earlier HubSpot site builds. Clients are not named. Each lesson changes how we plan this one.

  1. Plan tiers cap site pages.Product pages live in HubDB and dynamic pages, so the catalogue never competes with the page allowance. First-week task: confirm DMP's HubSpot tier and page limits first.
  2. Draft pages are not publicly viewable.So QA does not run on drafts. We review on a staged, noindex publish that anyone on the team can open.
  3. Theme fields accept only simple types.Header, footer and schema therefore become modules, not theme fields, so they can hold links, groups and structured data.
  4. Unsourced claims leak out of previews.Every claim sits on a to-confirm list until DMP signs it off. Nothing ships on the strength of a preview.
  5. Forms need owners before build.Routing owners, consent text and thank-you states are defined first, and the money path (form, owner, task, reply) is tested end to end before launch.
First-week task: confirm DMP's HubSpot tier and page limits. Tier decides dynamic pages, memberships, serverless functions and page allowances. Everything on this page assumes Content Hub Professional or higher, to confirm.
Regions and languages

US first, ready for the rest

A plan, not a commitment. The first release is the US site in English. The structure is built so international pages can follow without a rebuild.

US first

All templates, the catalogue and the dealer routes launch for the US. Region pages use the Dealer directory table.

hreflang-ready

The international distributor pages (8 distributors on the international page today) sit in a structure that can carry language and region variants, with hreflang pairs added when a translated page exists. We never publish an hreflang that points nowhere.

Translation workflow

Options to choose from later: HubSpot multi-language variants with translations by DMP or an agency, or machine draft with human review. Which one, and which languages, is to confirm.

Launch plan and QA

Nothing goes live until the gate list passes

The same checks as the quality standard, applied to the HubSpot build. The standard says what we measure; this is the order we do it in.

  1. Stage on a HubSpot address, noindex.The site is built and reviewed on a hs-sites address marked noindex, so reviewers see the real thing and search engines see nothing.
  2. Import the redirect map.Loaded from redirects.csv in bulk: 224 permanent (301) redirects and 17 removed-page (410) entries, per the current preview build. Tested after import, old URL to new URL to status.
  3. 404 page.A useful 404 with search and the three front doors, not a dead end.
  4. Robots, llms and sitemap package.robots.txt, llms.txt and the sitemap prepared together, released at launch and not before.
  5. Canonical and Open Graph rewrite.On launch day, scripts/launch_rewrite.py rewrites canonical and Open Graph addresses from the staging address to dmp.com, then the result is re-checked.
  6. Accessibility pass.axe-core at 390 and 1440 wide, plus a manual keyboard pass and a screen reader pass on the key journeys.
  7. Forms test records.One test submission per form, with who received it and when, recorded.
  8. Performance budget.Core Web Vitals measured on the staged URL, and the numbers reported against a budget agreed with DMP (figures to confirm).
