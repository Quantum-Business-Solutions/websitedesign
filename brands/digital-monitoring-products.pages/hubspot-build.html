Build it on one platform

Your website, your CRM and your dealers, on the same platform.

DMP is launching HubSpot CRM, marketing, sales and service with us. This page explains why the website belongs there too, what the build looks like, where HubSpot is not the strongest tool, and the questions worth putting to any bidder, including us.

The short version

Three reasons, and the honest limits

1

One record for every visitor

On HubSpot, forms, tracked downloads and page views land on the contact and company record the moment they happen. A dealer who reads a product page, then asks for a quote, is one timeline. On a separate CMS, that link is a connector someone has to build, secure and keep working.

2

The catalogue fits

dmp.com's sitemap lists 369 product pages and 26 categories. HubSpot's HubDB holds 10,000 rows per table, and pages generated from it do not count against the website page limit. A product table with a filterable listing is a normal build, not a stretch.

3

Less to look after

Hosting, the CDN and platform security updates are HubSpot's job, and its hosting providers are audited to SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001. Editors work in a drag-and-drop editor on theme modules we build once. There are no plugins to patch.

The honest limits. HubDB is a flat table, not a relational database, and faceted filtering is something we build rather than switch on. If DMP's long-term plan is a configurator or a compatibility matrix driven from a product information system, a developer-led CMS is the stronger base. The comparison below says where.
What the build looks like

The three directions, made real in HubSpot

Every page in the directions you are looking at maps to a HubSpot template, and each repeated block maps to a module the team can edit.

ThemeOne theme, the chosen direction, with modules for the hero, tabs, flow, timeline and cards. Your team edits copy and images without a developer.
CatalogueA HubDB table of products (name, category, family, description, spec sheet, image) renders every product page and category listing from one template each.
LibrarySpec sheets and guides stay as files. HubSpot cannot redirect file URLs, so file names are kept as they are today.
Forms to CRMDealer, specifier and owner forms create or update contacts, route to the right owner and start the right workflow.
AudiencesSmart content changes the home page and calls to action for dealers, specifiers and owners. Dealer-only content uses memberships (tier to confirm).

How we stage it

The site is built and reviewed on a HubSpot address marked noindex, then pinned to dmp.com on launch day. Nothing indexable changes until the redirect map has been tested.

How we protect what ranks today

A redirect map covers every URL that earns traffic, loaded in bulk (HubSpot takes up to 500 per CSV, 140 characters per URL). Product page addresses are kept wherever possible, lowercase.

Where Craft would be the better choice. A catalogue with deep relationships (compatibility, accessories, configurators), a fully headless front end, or a product information system as the source of truth. If the site is mostly marketing, a catalogue, documentation and lead capture that feed a CRM, HubSpot is simpler to run and to own.
Questions for any bidder

Put these to us as well

  • Who patches the CMS and its plugins, and how fast?
  • What is the plan for roughly 600 URLs and the existing rankings, and how is it tested before launch?
  • How do forms reach the CRM, and who fixes it when it breaks?
  • Who owns the structured data and keeps it correct as products change?
  • What does year two cost: hosting, licences, upgrades and support?
  • What happens to editing when the developer is not available?
Risks we would manage on HubSpot

What could go wrong

  • Catalogue architecture. Settle table design, filters and the 10-template limit before any page is drawn.
  • Tier. Dynamic pages and memberships need Content Hub Professional or Enterprise; confirm what DMP holds.
  • Lowercase paths. HubDB dynamic page paths must be lowercase, so mixed-case product URLs need mapping.
  • File URLs. Files cannot be redirected, so document names stay as they are.
  • Hand edits. Protect editors' changes from being overwritten by a rebuild.
Beyond the brief

What would make it better than any rebuild

A product finder

Filter the catalogue by system, panel family and use, from the HubDB table. Dealers find the part in two clicks.

A dealer-only library

Pricing sheets, brand assets and training behind memberships, with access recorded on the CRM record.
